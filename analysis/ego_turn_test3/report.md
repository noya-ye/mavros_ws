# ego_turn_test3 Analysis

Bag: `/home/jetson/mavros_ws/ego_turn_test3/ego_turn_test3_0.db3`, read-only analysis. Times are relative to bag start. No flight source/configuration or PX4 parameters changed.

## Observations

1. Raw EGO setpoints begin at 44.717 s. OFFBOARD remains active until the first recorded AUTO.LAND state at 74.792 s. Raw messages continue to 84.519 s, including landing/disarmed intervals; those later messages must not be treated as active trajectory tracking.
2. The external position gain actually used is 0.7. This is inferred from the dot product of raw-minus-latest-PX4 position and the capped EGO-minus-latest-LIO position error. At gain 0.7 the position mapping residual is essentially zero for 95% of raw samples. Current YAML is 0.8, so it is not a parameter snapshot of this recording.
3. In the second turn at 65.719 s, actual PX4 Y velocity is +0.399 m/s, compared with EGO +0.180 m/s. EGO Y acceleration is already -0.097 m/s2 and raw position correction Y is -0.063 m. The combined controller still shows lateral overshoot/delayed braking before any hold switch.
4. At 66.485 s (21.778 s after EGO commands begin), the EGO command changes from XY=(3.866,0.753) to XY=(3.970,1.018): a 0.285 m jump in about 18 ms. Velocity changes from (0.182,0.164) m/s to zero; acceleration changes from (0.015,-0.088) m/s2 to zero. Trajectory ID stays 22. The position difference divided by time is not a real commanded velocity: the velocity fields explicitly become zero.
5. The new held XYZ=(3.970080,1.018487,0.630467) exactly matches the latest received FAST-LIO sample. Previously commanded Z was the planner's nominal 1.0 m. This is consistent with the current planner/traj-server emergency cancellation behavior, which copies measured XYZ into a hold command. Offboard EGO holds its own entry altitude, so that Z switch is evidence of the upstream hold mechanism, not a commanded altitude change to PX4.
6. Raw XY position target changes from (3.934,0.867) at 66.468 s to (4.010,1.065) at 66.518 s, a 0.212 m jump. Previously active negative Y position correction and negative Y acceleration feedforward disappear as the new hold position is substituted. Zero velocity feedforward is not a guarantee that physical velocity immediately becomes zero.
7. At the upstream switch, actual PX4 velocity is approximately (0.119,0.364) m/s. Actual LIO Y subsequently peaks at approximately 1.154 m, 0.136 m beyond the new hold Y. The stopped command persists for the rest of the bag; the original 8 m task is not completed in this recording.
8. A similar cancellation occurs at 52.285 s: EGO XY jumps 0.162 m to the latest measured LIO position, with velocity/acceleration zero. At 53.367 s, a new trajectory starts with a further 0.171 m position change. Normal periodic replacements between these events show small command changes; the hold transitions are different events.
9. Before the second stop, maximum simultaneous position tracking error is 0.294 m, P95 0.205 m. The previous bag had 0.538 m maximum error, but the routes and stop events differ, so this cannot establish that gain reduction alone improved tracking. Euclidean tracking error includes along-track timing lag and is not the same as perpendicular distance to a route. Near the end of this truncated command trace, even nearest-path distance includes missing future trajectory, so it should not be interpreted as a pure cross-track error either.
10. Raw publishing remains at 20 Hz with maximum gap 51.7 ms; EGO command reception has maximum gap 25.8 ms. Feedforward norms peak at 0.314 m/s and 0.176 m/s2, below current code limits. There is no evidence that command starvation or those caps caused the second event.
11. Latest LIO sample age at raw publication: median 113 ms, P95 163 ms. Delayed external position feedback can contribute to phase lag. Header age is an observation of timing, not a measurement of the entire PX4 feedback delay. Recorded estimator horizontal position/velocity flags remain valid; no accel error/GPS glitch flags are set.

## What Can Be Concluded

Two effects coexist: lateral overshoot before the stop, and an upstream trajectory-to-hold discontinuity during the turn. Lowering kp alone cannot address the latter. The hold moves its reference to the aircraft's measured position rather than preserving the original route, explaining why later tracking against the new command can look acceptable while the aircraft has stopped away from the intended route.

The exact cancellation trigger cannot be established: this bag has no `/rosout`, `/ego_2d_planner/emergency_stop`, B-spline, selected path, or occupancy grid. Current source can enter emergency state after repeated planning failure; obstacle/map changes and the specific failure need recorded evidence. This is not proven to be a spontaneous ordinary replan or a particular PX4 PID defect. ULog/internal PX4 setpoints and gains are also absent.

## Priorities

1. Diagnose upstream cancellation before further gain changes. Add `/rosout`, `/ego_2d_planner/emergency_stop`, `/ego_2d_planner/bspline_2d`, `/ego_2d_planner/selected_path`, and `/ego_2d_planner/occupancy_grid` to the existing recorder. Inspect FSM transition reason, PLAN_FAIL counts, and map/path safety at the event. Verify exact names/types with the running graph.
2. Preserve obstacle checks and emergency handling. Do not disable cancellation or reduce collision margins to avoid the turn event. If changing the stop implementation, use a dynamically feasible braking trajectory initialized from the current estimated position/velocity, checked against obstacles, with a stationary hold only after braking. Account for the additional stopping distance; blindly filtering/delaying emergency commands may be unsafe.
3. Address the pre-stop overshoot using PX4 ULog: compare internal velocity targets, actual velocity, attitude targets/actual attitude, thrust and integrator behavior. If the internal velocity target is aggressive, inspect position correction and position gain; if actual velocity overshoots a reasonable internal target, inspect velocity damping, attitude response and saturation. The bag does not justify a numerical PID adjustment.
4. Hold kp at a known value for the next comparison (0.7 was used here), and record the startup parameter values. Further reducing kp may weaken route recovery. At the same route, trial max_vel 0.20-0.25 m/s as one controlled experiment and verify the actual command values; do not assume soft optimizer limits are hard bounds.
5. Separately trial a 50 Hz offboard publishing rate, and investigate LIO timing. Repeating old LIO data faster will not remove its age.
